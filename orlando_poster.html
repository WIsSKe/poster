British Literature · Novel Poster

Orlando

Virginia Woolf · 1928 · subtitled “A Biography”

Central Argument

Through three hundred years, two sexes, and the costume of every age, Orlando keeps a single self formed at its earliest and deepest layer - an observing, poetic temperament. Each era rewrites the surface; the core does not merely survive, it actively reconstitutes itself under every blow.

Evidence I
The Hilltop Oak
Elizabethan · Ch. 1
On the hilltop"It was very high, so high that nineteen English counties could be seen beneath... Rivers could be seen, and pleasure boats gliding on them; and galleons setting out to sea; and armadas with puffs of smoke...; and forts on the coast; and castles among the meadows; and here a watch tower; and there a fortress..."
  • Vantage - from above, alone: physical detachment is the observer’s stance.
  • Imagery - the eye takes in all at once, spires, smoke, sound, the small beside the vast.
  • Syntax - the long, list-like sentence moves as the eye moves, pausing on the trivial - a poet’s attention.

The poetic core is already present at the start.

Evidence II
Nick Greene’s Scorn
17th century · Ch. 2
Writing for himself"He resolved to write not for fame, but in obscurity to please himself... What has praise and fame to do with poetry? Was not writing poetry a secret transaction, a voice answering a voice?"
  • Target - the blow lands not on love but on the poetic vocation - straight at the core.
  • Irony - Woolf makes Greene, not Orlando, the smaller figure.
  • Stays / falls - the imposed shell drops (the cult of poets, hunger for fame); fidelity to poetry remains, purified.

Struck at its centre, the core sheds what was imposed and survives, cleaner.

Evidence III
Calling the Self
1928 · Ch. 6
The selves piled up"...a great variety of selves to call upon... these selves of which we are built up, one on top of another, as plates are piled on a waiter's hand..." until she becomes "what is called... a single self, a real self."
  • Arc closes - the eye that scanned space now surveys 300 years of time: same instrument, new material.
  • Syntax - a roll-call, self summoned after self until the deepest answers.
  • Many → one - the selves are layers that build one governing self; plurality is its building material.

Among a thousand selves, the earliest, deepest one presides.

One poem across every century - “The Oak Tree.” Begun by an Elizabethan youth, finished by a woman in 1928: the unbroken thread that proves the creative temperament never snaps.
